Research program

Automated and AI-assisted vulnerability discovery is advancing faster than the control story around it. Most tooling optimizes for detection capability and treats authorization, scope, and blast-radius as operational concerns handled by the operator. My thesis argues the opposite: safety should be a first-class, engineered property of the system.

How can an automated vulnerability-discovery system be designed so that its safety constraints are structural — enforced by the architecture itself — rather than relying on operator discipline or post-hoc policy?

The safety rails

  • Authorization gating — nothing runs against a target without an explicit, checkable authorization.
  • Scope enforcement — the engine physically cannot reach outside declared scope.
  • Blast-radius & rate control — bounded, observable impact.
  • Verification before report — findings are validated, not just asserted.
  • Kill-switch — a single, reliable stop.
  • Tamper-evident audit — every action is accountable after the fact.

How it's studied

All experimentation runs against self-owned or explicitly authorized, reproducible lab targets — never live or third-party systems. The research measures the trade-off between how strict the rails are and how much discovery coverage you keep: the real cost of being safe. Weaponizable exploit code and detection-evasion techniques are explicit non-goals and are not published.
